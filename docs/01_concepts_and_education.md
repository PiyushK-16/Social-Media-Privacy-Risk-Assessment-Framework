# Concepts & Education (Sections 1, 3, 9-17, 37)

> Educational framework. Scores are based on **self-reported** answers and are **not** a guarantee that an account will or will not be compromised.

## 1. Key terms - simple and technical

| Term | A. Simple explanation | B. Technical explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **Social-media privacy** | You decide who can see what about you. | Controls over collection, visibility, sharing and secondary use of personal data on a platform (audience settings, discoverability, tagging, data sharing with apps). |
| **Digital footprint** | The trail of things about you online. | *Active*: data you deliberately publish. *Passive*: data generated by your activity (logs, metadata, inferred interests) depending on the service. This project scores only **self-reported** practices. |
| **Personal-information exposure** | Personal facts that strangers can see. | The set of identifying/contact attributes (phone, e-mail, birth date, workplace...) reachable by an audience wider than intended. |
| **Oversharing** | Posting more than is needed. | Disclosure whose marginal social value is lower than its aggregate risk when combined with other public data (aggregation effect). |
| **Social engineering** | Tricking people instead of hacking computers. | Manipulating human trust (urgency, authority, familiarity) to obtain credentials, codes, money or access. Public context makes pretexts more believable. |
| **Identity-related risk** | Someone pretending to be you or using your details. | Impersonation, account recovery abuse and identity-verification abuse enabled by exposed attributes (name + birth date + contacts). |
| **Location exposure** | Showing where you are or usually are. | Real-time location, geotags, check-ins, routine and travel posts. Real-time broadcast is higher risk than a post made *after* leaving, because it reveals presence and absence right now. |
| **Public profiles** | Anyone can look. | Visibility is not automatically unsafe, but it increases the amount of information *potentially accessible*. |
| **Historical posts** | Old posts still exist. | Old content can contain outdated-but-still-sensitive details (school, address area, habits) and is rarely reviewed. |
| **Regular review** | Settings change, so check them. | Platforms change defaults and add features; periodic review (every 3-6 months) keeps configuration aligned with intent. |
| **MFA** | A second proof that it is really you. | Requires a second factor (authenticator app/security key) so a stolen password alone is not enough to take over the account. |

## 3. Privacy vs Security
- **Privacy** controls how personal information is collected, shared, exposed and used.
- **Security** protects systems, accounts and information from unauthorized access or misuse.

**Strong account security does not equal strong privacy.** Examples:
1. A user with MFA and a unique password (secure) posts live locations publicly (poor privacy).
2. A private profile (good privacy) reuses one password everywhere (weak security).
3. A friend tags you in public holiday photos: your security is untouched, but your privacy is exposed by someone else.
4. A third-party quiz app gets broad permissions: it is a security *and* privacy issue (least privilege).

## 9. Profile visibility
Analysed values: PUBLIC / FRIENDS / PRIVATE plus search-engine visibility (A2), friends-list visibility (A3), discoverability by phone/e-mail (A4). A public profile is not "unsafe" by itself - it widens the audience, so every other detail matters more.

## 10. Personal-information exposure
Questions ask only *"Is this publicly visible? YES/NO"* (B1-B7). The app **never** asks for the number, e-mail, address or birth date itself - assessment without collection.

## 11. Location privacy
Real-time sharing (C1), geotags (C2), check-ins (C3), travel plans (C4), home/work inferable (C5), routines (D3). Posting a photo *after* leaving shows where you were, whereas live sharing shows where you are and that your home may be empty. No geolocation or tracking is performed.

## 12. Photo and metadata awareness
Photos can reveal location context, workplace, school, vehicles, badges, documents, screens, family and travel information. **EXIF** is metadata embedded in an image file (device model, timestamp, sometimes GPS coordinates). Many platforms strip it on upload, but not all channels do. `tools/metadata_tool.py` reads metadata **locally**, shows only fields present in the file, never uploads, and writes a clean *copy* (original untouched).

## 13. Social-engineering risk
Behaviours assessed: unknown connections (E1), unverified DMs (I2), unexpected links (I1), sharing verification codes (I3), giveaways (I4). The project contains **no attack scripts**.

## 14. Account security
MFA (G1), password reuse (G2), password manager (G3), login alerts (G4), sessions (G5), recovery info (G6). Privacy risk and account-security risk overlap but are not identical.

## 15. Third-party apps
Connected apps (H1), social sign-in (H2), unused integrations (H3). **Principle of Least Privilege**: an app should hold only the access it needs, for as long as it needs it. Review and revoke regularly.

## 16. Tagging & mentions
Anyone can tag (F1), tag review (F2), open mentions (F3). Another person's post can expose you even if you post almost nothing yourself.

## 17. Digital footprint
Old posts (J1), old accounts (J2), public comments (J3), settings reviews (J4). Recommend a periodic privacy review.

## 37. How oversharing can increase social-engineering risk (defensive awareness)
Individually harmless public facts can be **combined** to make a scam message feel genuine. Categories of context to be careful with: **employer, college, travel plans, family references, interests, public events**. A message that mentions real context feels trustworthy even when it is fake.

Defences: limit public context; verify unexpected requests through a *separate* channel; never share verification codes; treat urgency and secrecy as red flags; report impersonation. (No personalised attack messages are provided in this project.)
